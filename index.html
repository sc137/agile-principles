Student Field Guide

Learn Agile Without the Corporate Jargon

Ever worked on a college group project where everyone worked alone for weeks, merged everything the night before Demo Day, and half the app broke? That is what Agile was invented to fix.

Instead of betting everything on one giant plan up front, Agile means building in small, working slices, talking to teammates and users early, and adjusting quickly when reality changes.

The "Finals Cram" Way (Waterfall)

Spend weeks writing specs and building parts separately. Discover bugs and misunderstood requirements right before the deadline.

The "Iterative Lab" Way (Agile)

Ship a tiny working version in Week 1, test it with classmates, adapt to rubric updates, and polish steadily without all-nighters.

Agile Terms in Plain Student English

Sprint (Iteration)
A short, fixed timebox (usually 1–2 weeks) where your team finishes a small, working piece of the project you could demo right away.
Product Backlog
Your prioritized wish list of features and fixes. High-value essentials sit at the top; "nice-to-have" bells and whistles stay lower down.
User Story & Story Points
A feature written from a user's perspective ("As a student, I want...") paired with a rough effort estimate (1 = tiny, 5 = big/uncertain).
MVP (Minimum Viable Product)
The simplest working version of your app that solves the core problem—no dark mode or extra animations until the core actually works.
WIP Limit (Work In Progress)
A cap on how many tasks your team starts at once. Stopping starting and starting finishing prevents half-done work from piling up.
Retrospective ("Retro")
A 15-minute team check-in after a Sprint to ask: What went well? What tripped us up? What one thing will we tweak next week?
Technical Debt
Quick-and-messy shortcuts ("we'll clean up this code later") that slow your team down every time you add a new feature.
